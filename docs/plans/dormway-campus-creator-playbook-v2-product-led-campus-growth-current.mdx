# DormWay Campus Creator Playbook v2 (Current)

## Executive Summary
DormWay should run a **product-led campus density strategy** with creators as a feeder layer, not the primary growth engine.

V1 over-indexed on views and posting volume. V2 optimizes for:
- first actions (WFAU)
- retained users (D14/W4)
- campus density
- trust durability

This aligns with DormWay's core moat: reducing academic operational chaos with high trust and fast time-to-value.

## Why V2 Exists
The prior creator playbook is useful for top-of-funnel velocity, but it is not enough for durable growth.

Main gaps to fix:
- Views were treated as success, but views do not guarantee activation or retention.
- Brand-owned content at high volume can feel less authentic to Gen Z than creator-owned distribution.
- No pre-publish safety gate creates avoidable brand, compliance, and partnership risk.
- Daily volume incentives can degrade content quality and create creator burnout.
- Single-channel dependence (TikTok-first) is fragile.

## Strategy Thesis (Locked)
DormWay scales best when growth compounds at the campus level:
1. Students get immediate value from first action.
2. That value is shareable.
3. Sharing drives qualified friends into first action.
4. Lifecycle loops convert first action into weekly habit.
5. Multiple micro-networks on the same campus create density.

Creators are critical, but they are one part of this loop.

## Growth Architecture (3 Engines)

### Engine A: Campus Density (Primary)
Goal: concentrated adoption in target campuses, not shallow national reach.

Core channels:
- ambassadors / campus operators
- student org officers
- RA / peer mentor networks
- club-level setup events

Primary KPIs:
- WFAU by campus
- D14 and W4 by campus
- campus density (% active students in target segment)

### Engine B: Creator Feeder (Secondary)
Goal: drive qualified first actions with authentic student content.

Core channels:
- TikTok + Reels (cross-post)
- creator-owned channels + DormWay-owned campus handles
- high-utility short-form content

Primary KPIs:
- source-tagged first actions per creator
- cost per activated user
- D14/W4 quality of creator-sourced cohorts

### Engine C: Lifecycle Habit Loop (Retention)
Goal: reinforce weekly planning behavior without notification fatigue.

Core channels:
- Sunday preview
- one due-soon nudge
- setup recovery flow

Primary KPIs:
- Monday/Tuesday active lift
- D14/W4 delta in exposed cohorts
- opt-out and complaint rates

## Gen Z Positioning Rules
DormWay should feel like a high-agency utility, not a dependency product.

Message pillars:
- "Cancel the noise. Do the work."
- "We automate clerical chaos, not your effort."
- "You stay in control; DormWay provides receipts and clarity."

Do:
- lead with concrete outcomes ("know what's due", "what changed")
- show trust boundaries clearly
- feature real student workflows and constraints

Don't:
- lead with vague AI hype
- imply the app does schoolwork for students
- use humiliation/controversy as core brand identity

## Creator Program v2 Design

### Program Structure
Use a hybrid model:
- 50% creator-owned accounts (authenticity + creator upside)
- 50% DormWay-owned campus handles (continuity + institutional memory)

Unit of operation:
- campus pods of 3-5 creators, not isolated individuals
- one pod lead per campus

Cadence:
- quality cadence target: 3-5 high-signal posts/week/creator
- avoid mandatory daily posting baseline

### Content Mix (70/20/10)
- 70% utility content: setup, "what changed", weekly planning flow, real deadlines
- 20% identity/story: day-in-life, finals week reality, creator perspective
- 10% trend experiments: fast trend adaptation with strict guardrails

### Format Guardrails
Preferred:
- product-in-use clips
- before/after organization workflows
- "one action in 30s" demos
- campus-specific study logistics

Restricted/avoid:
- rage-bait or humiliating professor/peer content as core tactic
- academic integrity gray-zone framing
- unqualified product claims

## Compensation Model v2 (Outcome-Weighted)

### Compensation Components
1. Base content fee
- $20-$30/post for accepted posts that meet quality and compliance checks

2. Activation bonus (quality)
- bonus per attributed first action (within 7 days)
- suggested starting range: $2-$5 per first action

3. Retention bonus (durability)
- additional bonus per attributed user retained at D14 and W4
- suggested range: $4-$10 per retained activated user

4. Campus density bonus (network effect)
- monthly bonus when campus-level thresholds are hit (not just creator-level views)
- examples: WFAU threshold + D14 floor + referral share rate floor

5. Leadership bonus
- pod leads earn additional stipend tied to pod quality score

### Why This Model
- Pays creators for value, not only reach.
- Prevents over-paying for low-intent virality.
- Aligns creator behavior with DormWay's real growth loop.

## Measurement and Attribution Requirements
No channel scales without decision-grade instrumentation.

Required event backbone (must be trusted before scale):
- landing upload start/completion
- source and campaign tags on every creator link
- first action completion flags
- D7/D14/W4 cohort retention by source + campus
- referral-assisted first action attribution

Required weekly dashboard cuts:
- by campus
- by creator/pod
- by channel
- by first action type (upload/import/integration)

Decision rule:
- kill or iterate if activation/retention quality decays, even if top-of-funnel grows.

## Lightweight Brand Safety and Compliance System
Maintain speed, add a minimal safety layer.

### Required controls
- creator onboarding + signed agreement
- disclosure standard (`#Ad` and platform partnership tools where applicable)
- prohibited claims list
- rapid takedown and correction protocol
- random sample audit of published content

### Worker classification posture
- pay per deliverable/outcome, not fixed schedule control
- creators choose production process and tools
- avoid script-level control
- permit multi-brand work (subject to direct competitor boundaries)

### Tax and documentation
- W-9 collection before first payment
- annual 1099-NEC handling per current rules and advisor guidance
- maintain dated legal checklist and policy owner

## Campus Rollout Plan (90 Days)

### Phase 1 (Weeks 1-3): Foundation
- lock instrumentation gaps
- recruit 2 pilot campus pods
- deploy creator onboarding, agreements, and compliance materials
- launch content playbooks and tracking links

Exit criteria:
- attribution trusted
- minimum creator output achieved
- no unresolved compliance incidents

### Phase 2 (Weeks 4-8): Prove Repeatability
- run pod playbook on 2-3 campuses
- tune content mix by first-action conversion and retention quality
- stand up referral ask after first value

Exit criteria:
- stable WFAU by campus
- non-degrading D14 and W4 for creator-sourced cohorts
- positive campus density trend in pilots

### Phase 3 (Weeks 9-12): Controlled Expansion
- expand to 5-8 campuses only if phase gates pass
- hire additional pod leads
- standardize onboarding, scorecards, and lifecycle handoff

Exit criteria:
- repeatable economics across multiple campuses
- no major trust/compliance regressions

## Operating Cadence
Monday:
- review scorecard by campus/pod/channel
- pick 3 bets max (one per engine)

Tuesday-Thursday:
- ship content tests and lifecycle improvements
- monitor activation quality daily

Friday:
- keep/kill/iterate decisions
- trust/compliance audit
- update next-week briefs

## What We Stop Doing
- treating views/likes as primary success criteria
- scaling to new campuses before retention quality is stable
- daily volume mandates as default creator expectation
- high-risk controversy formats as core brand tactic

## What We Double Down On
- first-action speed and quality
- campus pods and local network effects
- referral moments after clear product win
- trust-forward messaging and proof surfaces

## Expansion Gates (Hard)
A campus expands only when all are true for 2+ consecutive measurement windows:
- WFAU meets threshold
- D14 and W4 are non-degrading
- referral-assisted first action rate meets threshold
- creator-sourced cohorts are not lower quality than baseline

## Risks and Mitigations
- Risk: high top-of-funnel, weak activation.
  - Mitigation: quality-weighted payouts and first-action optimization.
- Risk: creator churn.
  - Mitigation: hybrid ownership, pod support, leadership incentives.
- Risk: compliance incident.
  - Mitigation: minimal gate + rapid response protocol.
- Risk: platform volatility.
  - Mitigation: cross-platform distribution and campus-owned channels.

## Decision Ownership
- Growth owner: GTM performance, channel mix, campus expansion calls
- Product owner: first-action and retention UX improvements
- Operations owner: creator onboarding, payouts, compliance workflow
- Data owner: instrumentation integrity and weekly scorecard
