# Campus Priority List

DormWay's current campus strategy is deliberately narrow. The active operating plan says to focus on **5-8 target campuses**, build **10-15 Campus Leads** across that group, and avoid confusing broad awareness with local density.

<Note>
  The live operating focus is not the same thing as the older Top 50 expansion ranking. Current execution docs should win over older reference material when they disagree.
</Note>

---

## Current Execution Posture

These are the clearest campus-level truths in the current vault:

| Topic | Current truth |
|-------|---------------|
| **Overall scope** | Focus stays on 5-8 target campuses, not national spread. |
| **Beachhead experiments** | **UMich** and **UCLA** are the named beachheads in the April-August 2026 org plan. |
| **Campus Lead goal** | Build 10-15 Campus Leads across 5-8 campuses in May and June. |
| **Paid test campuses** | The latest paid-media reference dated April 14, 2026 names **UMich**, **UCLA**, and **South Carolina** in active geo-targeted Snapchat campaigns. |
| **August school type focus** | Residential semester schools first. Flexible-term expansion stays separate unless a pilot forces it. |
| **UCLA nuance** | UCLA ambassador contracts are treated as optional acceleration, not a dependency, in the current org plan. |

One important detail: the current source docs do **not** publish a final named list of all 5-8 active campuses beyond these explicit references. That is better represented as an in-progress operating set than as a frozen public roster.

---

## Why the Beachheads and the Top 50 List Do Not Match

There is a real mismatch in the vault, but it is explainable:

- The **current operating plan** chooses beachheads based on execution reality: founder access, existing density, current campus presence, and real activation potential.
- The **Top 50 expansion note** is a **draft** from **June 11, 2025** that ranks long-range expansion candidates by college-town characteristics and enrollment.
- That same older note explicitly treats **University of Michigan** as the launch base and proven integration model, so Michigan carries extra strategic weight there beyond its raw college-town score.

That is why **UMich** can be a current beachhead while sitting at **#19** in the older draft, and **UCLA** can be a current beachhead while sitting at **#49**. The two documents are solving different problems.

So the right way to read the campus strategy is:

1. Current execution: narrow, density-first, beachheads now.
2. Long-range sourcing reference: Top 50 list, useful for future expansion thinking.

---

## What the Top 50 Analysis Is Actually Ranking

The Top 50 note is trying to answer a different question than the current GTM docs: **where should DormWay expand once the campus-density playbook is ready to scale?**

These are the main dimensions called out in the analysis:

| Dimension | What the draft is optimizing for |
|-----------|----------------------------------|
| **Student population size** | Large undergraduate markets, with the scoring framework favoring schools above roughly 15,000 students. |
| **College-town characteristics** | Places where students make up a large share of the local population and campus culture dominates local life. |
| **Community engagement** | Strong traditions, athletics, Greek life, and campus identity that can amplify word of mouth. |
| **Competitive dynamics** | Schools with less campus-app saturation or weaker incumbent positioning. |
| **Geographic strategic value** | Adjacent campuses and regional clusters that make follow-on expansion easier. |

So the note is best understood as a **future sourcing model**, not as a live operating scoreboard.

## What the Analysis Adds

The useful part of the Top 50 note is not only the ranking. It also surfaces a few patterns worth keeping:

| Pattern | What the draft says |
|---------|----------------------|
| **Geographic spread** | The top 50 intentionally covers the Southeast, Midwest, West Coast, Northeast, and Southwest so DormWay can build regional footholds rather than one-off launches. |
| **Conference footprint** | The list emphasizes major conference ecosystems, especially the **SEC** and **Big Ten**, because those schools often have stronger campus identity and adjacency effects. |
| **Enrollment sweet spot** | Many Tier 1-2 schools fall in the **20,000-40,000 undergraduate** range: big enough to matter, small enough to stay campus-dominant. |
| **Hidden gems** | Schools like **Ole Miss**, **Ohio University**, and **UC Santa Barbara** stand out because the student population dominates local life more than many brand-name flagships. |
| **Urban caution** | Big names like **Ohio State**, **UCLA**, and **Washington** can still matter strategically, but the draft treats them as harder early markets because the surrounding city dilutes campus-specific behavior. |

### Regional Cluster Logic

One of the strongest ideas in the draft is that expansion should compound through adjacent clusters rather than scattered single-campus bets.

| Cluster | Representative campuses | Why it matters |
|---------|-------------------------|----------------|
| **Michigan / Indiana corridor** | UMich, Michigan State, Indiana, Purdue | Lets DormWay build on a Midwest proof point and expand through similar Big Ten dynamics. |
| **Southeast cluster** | Clemson, Auburn, Georgia, Alabama | Concentrates around high-spirit schools with strong campus identity and organic peer distribution. |
| **California chain** | UC Santa Barbara, Cal Poly SLO, UC Davis, UC Berkeley | Creates a West Coast path that moves from classic college towns toward larger brand-name campuses. |
| **Texas network** | Texas A&M, Texas Tech | Highlights the value of state-level adjacency even when markets differ in density. |

## How Current GTM Should Use This

The current campus-density playbook adds an important interpretive rule: **creators are a feeder layer, not the primary engine.** Campus density, first actions, and retention quality still win.

That means the Top 50 analysis is most useful for:

- identifying future cluster bets once a beachhead is working
- spotting college-town-heavy schools where DormWay may get better acquisition and retention economics
- separating high-potential density markets from high-profile but diffuse urban campuses

It should **not** be used as:

- a public claim about which campuses are live right now
- a substitute for current instrumentation, activation, or retention gates
- a reason to expand broadly before the 5-8 campus operating model is repeatable
