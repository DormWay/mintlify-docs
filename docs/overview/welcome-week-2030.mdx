# Welcome Week 2030

Welcome Week is the Class of 2030 activation window where DormWay turns pre-arrival interest into trusted weekly use. The reference frame is intentionally generic: it should work for any target campus, while still preserving the operating lessons from the 2026 freshman launch plan.

The core job is simple: be useful when incoming students are moving from abstract college anxiety into concrete schedules, roommates, dorm logistics, orientation events, and first assignments.

<Note>
  Welcome Week is not only a marketing moment. It is the handoff from incoming-student curiosity to real academic setup.
</Note>

---

## Operating Goal

By Welcome Week, DormWay should help a new student answer four practical questions:

| Question | DormWay answer |
|----------|----------------|
| **What is my first week going to feel like?** | First-week preview, schedule shape, campus context, and visible next steps. |
| **What do I need to do before classes start?** | Move-in checklist, orientation reminders, Canvas / schedule / syllabus setup, and recovery prompts. |
| **How do I coordinate with other people?** | Roommate or room-group coordination, schedule sharing, and group availability links. |
| **Why should DormWay stay on my phone?** | Real assignment dates, due-soon nudges, widgets, and a calm weekly view once classes begin. |

The success condition is not raw traffic. It is source-tagged first action, campus density, and retention after the first syllabus or schedule import.

## Student Timeline

| Window | Student moment | Product / GTM job |
|--------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Decision Day** | Students commit, compare expectations, and start asking what college will really be like. | Capture interest, answer orientation questions, and set up a low-friction follow-up path. |
| **May-June** | Students are registering, watching campus content, and looking for practical advice. | Provide course-selection guidance, schedule import prompts, pre-arrival email, and community entry points. |
| **July** | Housing and roommate assignments create real coordination needs. | Support roommate invites, room coordination, what-to-bring planning, and move-in preparation. |
| **Pre-move-in** | Students are packing, checking dates, and wondering what happens first. | Shift lifecycle messaging from curiosity to activation: "Syllabi are about to drop. Here's the move." |
| **Move-in week** | Phones are out, students are meeting people, and campus logistics are urgent. | Make DormWay visible through live demos, shareable cards, widgets, QR / AirDrop paths, and concrete setup help. |
| **Orientation week** | Students decide what tools are useful enough to keep. | Convert setup into first action, keep support close, and make the product feel human and trustworthy. |
| **Class start** | Real coursework begins and the product promise is tested. | Push syllabus ingest, due-soon nudges, weekly preview, group scheduling, and activation recovery. |

## Move-In Week Plan

Move-in week should feel logistical, not promotional. Students are carrying boxes, meeting roommates, navigating campus, and answering parent questions. DormWay should reduce friction in that moment.

| Need | Reference behavior |
|------|--------------------|
| **Logistics** | Show move-in weather, campus parking / unloading notes, dining hours, and orientation events when available. |
| **Room coordination** | Help roommates decide who brings what, store shared notes, and preserve invite membership after account creation. |
| **Setup path** | Make the next action obvious: import a schedule, connect Canvas, or forward syllabi to DormWay. |
| **Live help** | Use campus operators, ambassadors, or founder presence for demos and fast support during the highest-anxiety days. |
| **Share surface** | Make DormWay easy to show in person through Semester Cards, Founding Cards, widgets, and QR / deep-link previews. |

Move-in content should sound like practical peer help: what to bring, what not to overthink, what orientation will not tell you, and how to get set up before the first week turns chaotic.

## Welcome Week Product Stack

The Welcome Week stack should be narrow enough for a small team to operate, but complete enough to cover the actual student moments.

| Layer | Role |
|-------|------|
| **First-week preview** | Turns an abstract schedule into a concrete first Monday: classes, walks, weather, dining, and campus events where data exists. |
| **Move-in mode** | Checklist-driven campus logistics for the August 10-25 style arrival window. |
| **Semester Card** | A shareable answer to "what classes are you taking?" during dorm and orientation conversations. |
| **Class Timeline Share** | A practical artifact for class GroupMe / Discord threads once assignments and dates exist. |
| **iOS Widgets** | Passive visibility on lock screen and home screen throughout Welcome Week. |
| **Find a Time** | A public availability link for study groups, clubs, and early friend groups. |
| **Founding Card** | A lightweight identity / QR surface for orientation handoff and early-user pride. |
| **Activation recovery** | Email, in-app, and support follow-up for students who signed up but have not completed first action. |

Nothing in this stack should require a giant ambassador program or heavy manual operations. The default bar is two-founder operability plus tightly scoped campus help.

## Launch Readiness

Before expanding Welcome Week distribution, the following gates should be true:

| Gate | Requirement |
|------|-------------|
| **Trust** | Landing, onboarding, privacy, and help copy make clear what DormWay reads, what it does not do, and what students get first. |
| **Instrumentation** | Source, campus, signup, and first-action events are queryable enough to separate vanity traffic from activated users. |
| **Flags** | Every Welcome Week surface has a controlled rollout path and a fast kill switch. |
| **Support** | Founder / operator coverage is defined for day 1-3, with a clear escalation path for privacy, incorrect course data, and public-surface abuse. |
| **Moderation** | Public surfaces with free text have review or suppression paths before scaling. |
| **Product quality** | Schedule import, syllabus ingest, Canvas connection, and first-run help pass a real pre-launch dry run. |

If any of these gates fails, reduce the launch surface rather than pushing a confusing experience into a high-trust moment.

## Metrics To Watch

Welcome Week should be measured by quality and density:

| Metric | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|
| **Source-tagged WFAU** | Confirms the campaign is producing users who complete a real first action within seven days. |
| **Activation rate by source and campus** | Separates useful channels from channels that only create shallow signups. |
| **D7 / D14 retention** | Shows whether move-in attention becomes weekly academic behavior. |
| **Time to first value** | Measures whether students reach a useful outcome before they lose trust. |
| **Support burden** | Keeps the launch operable for a small team. |
| **Public-surface conversion** | Tracks whether share cards, widgets, and scheduling links create real account and first-action movement. |

## Operating Rules

- Lead with practical student utility, not "AI-powered" positioning.
- Keep the primary promise stable: **Never wonder what's due.**
- Treat move-in and orientation as trust-building moments, not vanity launch theater.
- Ship fewer surfaces if that protects reliability and clarity.
- Keep public artifacts honest about extracted data and confidence.
- Use paid, ambassador, or campus-lead activity only when attribution and onboarding quality are readable.
- Stop work that creates signups without first action, support confusion, or manual load the team cannot sustain.

The durable pattern is the freshman event spine: Decision Day, schedules, roommates, move-in, orientation, and class start. Welcome Week works when those moments feel like one continuous setup path rather than a collection of unrelated campaigns.
