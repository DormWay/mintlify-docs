# Class of 2030 Landing Page

The Class of 2030 landing page is a draft design reference for `dormway.app/2030`. Its job is to meet incoming freshmen before there is a concrete urgent to-do, reduce cognitive load, and route them into the right pre-arrival product surface when the season changes.

<Warning>
  This page reflects a draft design note from Obsidian. Riley sign-off is required before canonical claim copy, competitive positioning, or final public-page copy ships.
</Warning>

The strategic frame is not "readiness dashboard" or "activation funnel." It is a pre-emptive orientation surface: the page should reassure students that most of what they have been told to get ready for is not urgent yet, then give them one simple action.

---

## Page Principle

The page must answer one question at every level:

> Does this reduce cognitive load and restore orientation, or does it add another thing to manage?

That rule leads to a tight public-page shape:

| Principle | Landing-page implication |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| **One screen** | The public page should not ask freshmen to reason through a long product roadmap. |
| **One truth** | "You just committed. Most of what you have been told to get ready for is not urgent yet." |
| **One action** | A single `Save your spot` CTA across seasonal variants. |
| **Quiet future promise** | Future moments appear as "what you will see when you need it," not as separate CTAs. |
| **No performance framing** | No counters, ranks, leaderboards, sticky CTA bars, or founding-status pressure above the fold. |

## Recommended Public Structure

The page should stay compact and calming:

| Section | Role |
|---------|------|
| **Hero** | Class of 2030 eyebrow, time-aware H1, short relief-oriented subcopy, Riley founder line, one primary CTA. |
| **Trust line** | Works across thousands of schools, free for students, privacy / FERPA posture where appropriate. |
| **What you will see, when you need it** | Four-line timeline: now, June, July, August. No buttons. |
| **FAQ** | Collapsed by default; answers trust, privacy, school coverage, and what happens after saving a spot. |
| **Footer** | Standard DormWay footer and policy links. |

The internal product progression can be richer than the public page. The public page should not expose all of that complexity at once.

## Time-Aware Hero Logic

Same URL, same action, seasonally different headline:

| Window | H1 | Primary CTA |
|--------|----|-------------|
| **May 1-14** | "You just committed. Most of what you've been told to 'get ready' isn't urgent yet." | Save your spot |
| **May 15-Jun 30** | "Registering for fall? Your semester can have a shape before classes start." | Save your spot |
| **Jul 1-Aug 10** | "Got your room? Your roommate doesn't have to be a stranger." | Save your spot |
| **Aug 11+** | "Syllabi just landed. You shouldn't have to wonder what's due." | Save your spot |

The CTA stays stable. What changes is the product surface that opens after signup: May can land in Class Mix, July can emphasize room coordination, and August can emphasize syllabus parsing.

## Mix Model

The design note treats Mix as the landing-page wedge because it can cover the pre-arrival social arc without creating separate products:

| Layer | Approximate scope | When it turns on | Student job |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|-------------|
| **Class Mix** | Full incoming class | May / Decision Day | See who else from the school is here and start low-stakes threads. |
| **Dorm Mix** | Residence hall or building group | Housing release | Coordinate around the people who will share space. |
| **Roommate Mix** | 2-4 people | Housing release | Decide what to bring, share notes, and reduce roommate uncertainty. |

The moat is behavioral data over self-reported identity. DormWay should use real school, schedule, and later syllabus context instead of asking students to perform a social profile.

## Class Mix Seeding Reference

The draft plan recommends a constrained seeding model:

| Item | Reference plan |
|------|----------------|
| **Scope** | 5-8 schools, Class of 2030 cohort only. |
| **Cadence** | One Riley-voice prompt per school every 2-3 days. |
| **Owner** | Community Lead, with Riley prompt approval. |
| **Effort** | Roughly 4-6 hours per week on top of existing community work. |
| **School strategy** | Seed where organic density matters; do not treat every school like a manually operated community. |

Prompt style should be low-effort and relief-oriented: a question with a built-in admission, not an identity-performance prompt.

## Empty State

For schools without enough activity, the page should not fake a crowd or turn early arrival into a status game.

| Mix size | Recommended behavior |
|----------|----------------------|
| **Fewer than 5 members** | Hide the feed. Say the student is early, DormWay will email when more people from the school are in, and avoid extra notifications. |
| **5-19 members** | Show the feed without public engagement metrics. |
| **20+ members** | Standard feed view, still avoiding likes, follower counts, view counts, or engagement-velocity sorting. |

The empty state is a relief surface, not a conversion surface.

## Telemetry Checklist

The draft landing-page plan depends on knowing whether Mix actually improves activation:

| Event / lens | Why it matters |
|--------------|----------------|
| `class_mix_view` | Tracks anonymous and authenticated demand by school and post count. |
| `class_mix_join` | Measures Class Mix entry by school and seasonal timing. |
| `class_mix_post` | Separates first-time posting from repeat activity. |
| `class_mix_reply` | Captures thread depth and whether prompts produce real interaction. |
| `class_mix_referrer` | Ties activity back to the public page, share links, or other entry paths. |
| **D7 retention by school and seed status** | Shows whether seeded schools retain better than unseeded schools. |
| **Schedule-import lift among Mix joiners** | Load-bearing metric. If Mix joiners do not import schedules at a higher rate, the social-academic loop is not working. |

## Guardrails

- Do not use live counters, ranks, founding status, or "help unlock your school" mechanics on the public page.
- Do not make students choose between Mix, schedule, roommate, and syllabus paths before they have value.
- Do not lead May visitors with "Never wonder what's due" unless the syllabus moment is actually active.
- Do not name or imply competitors in the headline; the product should feel calmer by itself.
- Keep Class Mix constrained: no likes, reactions, follower counts, view counts, notification firehose, or engagement sorting.
- Treat Roommate Mix as invite-based unless product and legal explicitly decide otherwise.

## Open Decisions

Before this becomes implementation-ready, the source note calls out several decisions:

| Decision | Why it matters |
|----------|----------------|
| **Invite-based vs. matching-based Roommate Mix** | Copy, trust posture, and legal posture differ sharply. |
| **Identity verification** | `.edu` email versus inherited DormWay auth changes trust and friction. |
| **Posting affordances** | Text-only keeps the surface calm; media-first pushes toward performative social behavior. |
| **Moderation scope** | Class Mix needs the same public-input discipline as other PLG surfaces. |
| **Parent block placement** | Parent revenue should not damage the student trust moment. |

The durable design direction is clear: `dormway.app/2030` should be the calmest page in the freshman feed. It should close tabs, not open them.
