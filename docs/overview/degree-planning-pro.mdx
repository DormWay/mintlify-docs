# Degree Planning Pro

DormWay's current Pro wedge is degree planning grounded in real institutional documents. The free layer answers requirement and catalog questions with citations. The paid layer sells the conversation, the scenario modeling, and the living graduation plan.

<Note>
  Current product rule: **the catalog is the free-tier anchor. The conversation is Pro.**

  Safety rule: every plan should ship with **"verify with your counselor"** front and center.
</Note>

---

## Product Definition

| Topic | Current truth |
|-------|---------------|
| **Core input set** | Degree audit, catalog, transcript or transfer-credit docs, plus the student's own preferences and constraints. |
| **Free value** | Requirement lookup, degree progress, catalog-grounded Q&A, and course-search help should work before the long intake flow. |
| **Pro value** | Students pay for the guided intake, semester-by-semester plan, scenario modeling, and re-planning over time. |
| **Positioning** | DormWay is not trying to replace Degree Works. It is an advisor-amplifier that helps students show up prepared. |
| **Coverage strategy** | Start from public catalogs, then deepen with student-uploaded audits and school-specific documents. |

The wedge matters because it extends DormWay from "what matters this semester?" into "am I actually on track to graduate?"

---

## Source Precedence

The source note is clear about which documents win when they disagree:

| Source | Current role |
|--------|--------------|
| **Degree audit** | Primary source for what the institution believes the student has completed or still owes. |
| **Catalog** | Primary rule system for requirements, policies, catalog-year obligations, and footnotes. |
| **Transcript / transfer-credit evaluation** | Primary source for externally earned credit that has not landed cleanly in the audit. |
| **Department roadmaps / advising sheets** | Secondary operational guidance for sequencing and local practice. |
| **Student answers** | Primary source for preferences, work hours, study-abroad plans, AP credit, and other off-system realities. |

That precedence is what makes the feature more than a course-search tool. It is a grounded planning system with explicit trust boundaries.

---

## Free vs. Pro Split

### Free layer

- Degree progress view
- Degree brief / gotcha detector
- "Does this class count?" answers with catalog citations
- Requirement-aware course search
- Catalog-year awareness
- Prereq chain checker
- Catalog Q&A on rules like repeat policy, late withdrawal, and double counting

### Pro layer

- Intake conversation that captures the things the school system does not know
- Semester-by-semester graduation plan
- Scenario modeling for summer school, lighter terms, or adding a minor
- What-if major change support
- Counselor prep sheet
- Re-planning after grades, schedule changes, or new constraints

The cleanest way to say it is: free explains the rules, Pro helps the student make decisions inside them.

---

## Why the Catalog Matters

The "course catalog stuff" is not a side detail. It is the wedge:

- the catalog contains the actual rule system
- the degree audit tells DormWay where the student currently stands
- the student supplies the missing real-world constraints that institutional systems usually do not know

That is why the one-sheet calls out a three-part model:

1. Institutional rules from the catalog
2. Student-specific status from the audit and transcript
3. Personal reality from the intake conversation

The note also points to a practical coverage plan: bootstrap from roughly **150 public catalogs**, then reuse parsed school-year data across students instead of redoing the work every time.

---

## Guardrails

- DormWay should lead with **"verify with your counselor"**, not bury it in fine print.
- The product is an advisor-amplifier, not academic advising in disguise.
- This is not a registration bot, a GPA optimizer, or a replacement for institutional systems.
- Pro should arrive after students already understand DormWay's value, which is why the Class of 2030 brief places the soft launch after August activation.

That framing keeps the feature ambitious without overclaiming what the product can responsibly promise.
