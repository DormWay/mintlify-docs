# Today Screen Product Principles

Durable principles for DormWay's Today surface across all platforms. These govern current implementation and future iterations. They should change rarely.

## What Today Is For

The Today screen answers one question: **"Am I okay — what do I need to deal with?"**

Not "what does my day look like." Not "here's everything we know about you." The job is awareness and confidence. A student should be able to open DormWay, look at Today, and close it knowing they are not forgetting anything that matters right now.

## What Today Is Not For

- Not a dashboard or command center
- Not a discovery surface or content feed
- Not a planning tool (that's a different screen, probably iPad/Mac-first)
- Not a productivity coach or study scheduler
- Not a place to browse ambient content

---

## Principles

### 1. Awareness Over Display

**What it means:** Today tells you what needs attention, not just what exists on your calendar. The difference between "you have 4 classes today" and "you have a problem set due at 5pm and you're free until 2:30" is the difference between a calendar and a second brain.

**Why it matters for real students:** Students don't open DormWay to see a pretty timeline. They open it because they're anxious about forgetting something, or they need to know what's next in the 30 seconds between leaving one class and walking to another. The screen must resolve that anxiety fast.

**UI/UX implication:** Urgent or attention-requiring items must be visible immediately, not buried in a chronological list. The screen should lead with what matters, not with the first event of the morning.

**Anti-pattern it rules out:** A timeline that shows 8am-10pm regardless of what actually needs attention. A screen that looks the same whether you have zero things due or five things due.

### 2. Honesty Over Comfort

**What it means:** The screen must never create false confidence. If the hero says "Free until 2:30" but there's a paper due at 5pm, the student must see both facts — not just the calming one. If DormWay doesn't have full visibility into a student's obligations, it should say so rather than implying everything is clear.

**Why it matters for real students:** The worst thing DormWay can do is make a student feel safe when they're not. One missed deadline because DormWay said "you're done for today" and the student believed it — that's a trust-destroying event. College is inconsistent. Professors change things. DormWay's value is being the reliable source, which means being honest about uncertainty.

**UI/UX implication:** When urgency and orientation conflict (e.g., "Free until 2:30" but "3 due today"), urgency must be visible alongside orientation, not hidden by it. When data coverage is partial, the screen should communicate what it knows and doesn't.

**Anti-pattern it rules out:** A hero state priority cascade where temporal orientation always wins over urgency. A "You're done for today" state that doesn't account for pending assignments.

### 3. Finite and Calm

**What it means:** The Today screen should have a bottom. A student should be able to scroll to the end and feel "that's everything." The screen should not invite endless browsing, open-ended discovery, or "what else can I do?" engagement mechanics.

**Why it matters for real students:** A stressed student with 4 things due does not need more things on their screen. An ADHD student who opened the app to check one thing does not need a swipeable card stack pulling their attention. The value of Today is that it is bounded — you see your situation, you act on what matters, you leave.

**UI/UX implication:** The screen has clear visual termination. No infinite scroll. No feed-style discovery mechanics. When there's nothing left to show, the empty state should feel like a reward ("you're clear"), not like something is missing.

**Anti-pattern it rules out:** Swipe card stacks, ambient feed cards, "you might also like" suggestions, or any mechanic designed to increase time-on-screen rather than resolve a need.

### 4. Priority Before Chronology

**What it means:** The most important information should be at the top, not the earliest event of the day. "3 assignments due" matters more than "9am lecture" if it's currently 2pm. Chronology is useful for orientation (your day backbone), but it's secondary to what actually needs your attention.

**Why it matters for real students:** Students don't think chronologically about their obligations. They think in terms of urgency: "what's going to bite me if I ignore it?" A timeline that starts at 8am and puts the urgent thing at 4pm is making the student do the triage work that DormWay should do for them.

**UI/UX implication:** Attention items (due today, conflicts, things that changed) appear above or more prominently than the chronological timeline. The timeline supports orientation ("here's your day") but doesn't dominate the screen.

**Anti-pattern it rules out:** A pure chronological timeline as the primary organizational structure, where urgency is just one card type mixed in with everything else.

### 5. Seconds, Not Minutes

**What it means:** The primary use case is a 5-15 second check. The screen must deliver its core value — "am I okay, what's next?" — in that window. Detailed information (expanded cards, course details, assignment specifics) is available on tap, but the default view answers the question at a glance.

**Why it matters for real students:** Students check between classes, walking across campus, in the 30 seconds before a lecture starts. They're not sitting down for a planning session (that's a different job on a different device). If they have to scroll or read to figure out their situation, the screen is too slow.

**UI/UX implication:** Hero state + stat pills + top attention items are visible without scrolling. Everything below the fold is supporting context, not primary information. Cards are collapsed by default, expandable on demand.

**Anti-pattern it rules out:** A screen where you must scroll past a large hero section to find out what's actually due. Expanded cards as the default state. Any layout where the critical information is below the fold.

### 6. Trust Is Earned by Accuracy, Not Features

**What it means:** A student trusts Today because it was right yesterday and the day before, not because it has a lot of features. Every piece of information on the screen that could be wrong erodes trust. Every speculative or generated element (AI copy, suggested blocks, inferred states) is a trust risk that must earn its place through accuracy over time.

**Why it matters for real students:** College is already full of systems that promise to help and then get it wrong — Canvas notifications that don't match reality, calendar events that are outdated, syllabi that were changed after being published. DormWay's bar is higher: if it's on the Today screen, it should be right. If it might not be right, it should be marked as uncertain.

**UI/UX implication:** Deterministic information (class times, due dates from Canvas) gets full confidence treatment. Speculative information (AI-suggested study blocks, LLM-generated copy) should either be absent from Today or visually distinguished as suggestions.

**Anti-pattern it rules out:** LLM-generated hero subtitles that could say something slightly wrong. Study block suggestions presented with the same visual weight as confirmed classes. Any information that might make a student second-guess whether to trust the screen.

### 7. Same Brain, Different Density

**What it means:** The Today surface should be the same conceptual experience across iPhone, iPad, and Mac — same data, same priority model, same answer to "am I okay?" — but the density and interaction model should adapt to each platform.

**Why it matters for real students:** Students use multiple devices. If Today looks completely different on iPhone vs. iPad vs. the web, it breaks the mental model. But if iPad shows the exact same single-column phone layout, it wastes the screen and misses the opportunity to support desk-based study sessions.

**UI/UX implication:** iPhone: single column, glanceable, 5-15 second check. iPad: can show more context (timeline + tasks side by side). Mac: densest, can include week view and task management alongside today. The underlying data model and priority logic is shared.

**Anti-pattern it rules out:** Building completely different architectures per platform (widget grid on web, fused timeline on iOS). Ignoring iPad/Mac entirely. Making the phone layout stretch to fill a 13" screen.

### 8. The Default Must Be Great. Customization Is Available, Never Required.

**What it means:** The Today screen works beautifully out of the box with zero configuration. The default is an opinionated timeline that shows your day, your urgency, and your status — and it's good enough that most students never touch a setting. But students who want to rearrange, resize, show, or hide sections can. The architecture supports customization. The product doesn't depend on it.

**Why it matters for real students:** The students who need DormWay the most — overwhelmed, anxious, behind — are the least likely to configure a dashboard. They need to open the app and immediately see if they're okay. But every student IS different: different majors, different professors, different rhythms, different priorities. A CS student and a film student and a pre-med student might all want different things prominent. The ones who care enough to customize should be able to. The ones who don't should get a default so good they never need to.

**UI/UX implication:** iPhone defaults to a tall vertical timeline (Hero + attention items + timeline + coming up). That's the product for 80%+ of users. Customization (reorder sections, resize the timeline, show/hide weather or nudges, pin a course) is available but never gates first value. On iPad/Mac where there's more screen space, customization becomes more valuable — spatial arrangement of widgets alongside the timeline makes sense at those sizes.

**Anti-pattern it rules out:** A blank canvas that asks students to "build their dashboard" before seeing anything useful. A settings page with 40 toggles. An onboarding flow that requires layout choices. But also: a locked screen with no ability to adapt it to how you actually think about your day.

## The Test

Before any change to the Today screen ships, ask:

1. Does a student get their answer in under 10 seconds?
2. Does the screen ever say "you're fine" when the student isn't?
3. Can a stressed student look at this and feel less overwhelmed, not more?
4. Is everything on this screen trustworthy right now, or are we guessing?
5. Does the screen end?
