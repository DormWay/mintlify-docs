# Class of 2030 Workback Plan - March to August 2026

> **If focus becomes unclear, default to this: Make DormWay the most trustworthy, fastest-to-value academic clarity tool for one concentrated freshman cohort by August 2026. Everything else is secondary.**

## Purpose
Keep DormWay focused on one thing:

**Win a dense, high-quality freshman cohort by the start of Fall 2026 without breaking trust, product quality, or the brand.**

This is a founder working doc, not an automatically promoted execution doc. It should inform weekly decisions, not create extra parallel projects.

## Core Decision

DormWay should **not** try to win Summer 2026 through broad paid acquisition, channel sprawl, or a pile of new programs.

DormWay should win by doing four things well:

1. Telling the right story.
2. Delivering first value fast.
3. Building trust at every step.
4. Concentrating effort in one dense freshman wedge, then expanding.

## Two Clocks Are Running

DormWay is operating against two real deadlines:

1. **Fundraising clock:** target a primary seed lead by **May 15, 2026** so a round can realistically come together during **June 2026**.
2. **Freshman clock:** be conversion-ready for incoming students well before move-in and the start of Fall 2026.

This means the company cannot wait until summer to clarify the story, prove the product loop, or prepare investor materials.

## August 2026 Target State

By early August 2026, DormWay should be able to say:

- We have a concentrated freshman cohort, not just scattered signups.
- New students can understand DormWay in under 30 seconds.
- New students can get first value in one primary action.
- The first-run experience feels trustworthy, guided, and calm.
- We know which acquisition sources produce first action and retention.
- We have at least one strong campus loop that is repeatable.
- We have enough retained student behavior and quality signals to tell a credible fundraising story.

## What Success Actually Means

Primary scoreboard:

- WFAU (weekly first-action users)
- activation
- D7 retention
- D14 retention
- W4 retention

Secondary scoreboard:

- time-to-first-value
- source-to-first-action quality
- freshman campus density
- referral-assisted first actions
- Sunday preview engagement

Quality bar:

- No scaled channel activity until source attribution is trusted.
- No major trust contradiction across landing, onboarding, lifecycle, FAQ, and pitch.
- No product surface goes live if it creates obvious first-run confusion.

## Strategic Interpretation

### 1. Class of 2030 is a wedge, not a second company

Use class-of-2030 positioning to capture admitted and incoming students early, but do **not** fork the company into a large parallel product line before the core product loop is solid.

Near-term interpretation:

- launch a narrow freshman-facing landing and content layer
- capture interest and email/SMS permission
- offer useful pre-arrival utility where it is fast and credible
- transition students into the main DormWay academic workflow as soon as classes, schedules, and syllabi exist

Do not spend the spring building a giant incoming-freshman product branch, comparison engine, full "tour guide OS", or a complex new CRM operation.

### 2. Quality beats quantity until quality is visible in the data

The right growth story is not "we drove a large volume of shallow signups."

The right growth story is:

- DormWay speaks to a real freshman pain point
- students get value quickly
- they come back weekly
- they tell friends because it actually helped

### 3. Founder story is the growth engine

Riley is not a side character in the GTM. Riley is a primary asset.

The best-performing framing should come from:

- lived student pain
- missed-deadline panic
- fragmented systems
- health, stress, burnout, and real chaos
- "I built what I needed"

This should shape landing copy, content, onboarding, and investor narrative.

### 4. Riley and Ethan need a clear operating split

The company needs a cleaner decision split so execution gets faster, not noisier:

- **Riley** (product / market / brand) owns: brand voice, positioning, landing copy, onboarding UX acceptance, GTM priorities, freshman narrative, and "should we ship this?"
- **Ethan** (technical / platform / ops) owns: backend reliability, integrations, data correctness, workflows, analytics implementation, and "can we ship this safely?"

No net-new student-facing feature should ship without passing both tests.

## What Not To Do Between Now and August

Do not:

- expand paid acquisition before attribution and onboarding quality are trusted
- build broad ambassador, creator, and labs systems that create management overhead without proven user-quality lift
- let short-form become the primary engine
- launch student-facing monetization messaging in core flows
- add novelty features that do not improve first value, trust, retention, or density
- build a large class-of-2030 product branch before a narrow freshman wedge proves out

## Decision Filters

Before starting any new project, ask:

1. Does this improve first value?
2. Does this improve trust?
3. Does this improve retention?
4. Does this increase freshman density in a target cohort?
5. Can Riley and Ethan realistically operate it through August?

If the answer is "no" to most of these, do not do it now.

## Explicit Cut List

Cut or pause by default:

- large creator program management
- large labs program expansion
- broad ambassador bureaucracy
- vanity-heavy influencer campaigns
- complicated student social systems
- speculative calculators and side features
- national expansion framing
- investor-facing traction theater unsupported by retention quality
