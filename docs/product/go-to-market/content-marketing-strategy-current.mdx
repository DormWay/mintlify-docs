# Content Marketing Strategy

## Executive Summary

A three-tier content system that leverages Riley's professional filmmaking equipment, comedy instincts, and founder story to produce cinematic short-form content for Instagram Reels and TikTok. The strategy replaces the prior 15-second ad-concept approach with narrative-driven, entertainment-first content where DormWay is the satisfying payoff — never the pitch.

The system is designed to:
- Produce 1-2 weekly episodes sustainably alongside product work
- Generate 1-2 hero pieces per month for viral reach (ramp to 2-3 once workflow is dialed)
- Map directly to the Class of 2030 workback plan timeline (March–August 2026)
- Operate within a two-founder company without external content hires

---

## Strategic Foundation

### Why This Approach

The existing content docs (Strong Ad Concepts, Content Schedule for Riley, Riley's Channel Reactivation) were written for an earlier moment — a January mobile launch, mystery-building around "where Riley went." That narrative window has passed. The [Class of 2030 Workback Plan - March to August 2026 (Draft)](/docs/product/go-to-market/class-of-2030-workback-plan-march-to-august-2026-draft) establishes the current strategic frame:

- **Founder story is the growth engine.** Riley is a primary asset, not a side character.
- **Short-form is a feeder only.** Content drives people to DormWay; it doesn't replace the product loop.
- **Quality beats quantity** until quality is visible in the data.
- **Campus density over national spread.** Content should resonate with real students at real schools, not chase broad virality.

### The Core Insight

The reference reel (an artist who opens with fake security-cam "crime" footage, then reveals himself as a painter using the hook to show his craft) demonstrates a content formula that works for Gen Z:

1. **Entertainment first, product second.** The content is worth watching even if you never download the app.
2. **Self-aware meta humor.** Transparency about "selling something" builds trust rather than eroding it.
3. **Misdirection as a hook.** Genre parody (true crime, heist, nature doc) stops thumbs because it's unexpected.
4. **Craft as the payoff.** The product reveal earns respect because the content earned attention first.

### Brand Guardrails Applied

Per the [DormWay Framing Guardrails (Current)](/docs/product/brand/dormway-framing-guardrails-current):
- DormWay is **crisis prevention infrastructure**, not a productivity tool for overachievers.
- Voice is **human, student-native, concrete, trust-forward.**
- Lead with **student pain**, not features.
- AI language: **outcomes first**, never "AI-powered."
- The Riley Test applies to every piece: Would Riley send this to a friend? Does it sound like a real student made it?

The comedy voice layers on top: **absurdist premises delivered deadpan, grounded in real student pain, with self-deprecating honesty.** The character is "the helpful older classman" — not a company, not a brand, just a guy who's been through it and built something.

## The Content System

### Three Tiers, One Voice

```
Tier 2 (weekly) builds the audience →
Tier 1 (hero) brings in new people →
Tier 3 (tentpole) converts at key moments →
Repeat
```

### Tier 1: "Mockumentary" Hero Pieces

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Purpose** | Viral reach — the videos strangers share |
| **Frequency** | 1-2 per month (ramp to 2-3 once workflow is dialed) |
| **Length** | 45-90 seconds |
| **Production** | Half-day to full-day shoot. Full cinema rig. |
| **Edit time** | 4-8 hours in Premiere + After Effects |

**Structure:** Misdirection hook (0-5s) → cinematic escalation of absurd premise (5-35s) → self-aware meta turn (35-45s) → DormWay reveal as satisfying payoff (45-60s)

**DormWay presence:** Product appears in the final 10-15 seconds only. Never pitched, always revealed.

**Production gear:**
- Camera: Canon R5 II
- Lenses: Sirui Venus anamorphics (35mm wide establishing, 50mm mid shots, 75mm close-ups) for distinctive cinematic look — horizontal flares, oval bokeh, widescreen ratio
- Stabilization: Edelkrone HeadPLUS v2 PRO for controlled pan/tilts, JibONE v2 for reveal moves, Zhiyun WEEBILL 3S for handheld motion
- Lighting: Neewer CB200C + CB100C for dramatic setups, Godox V1 Pro for accent/flash effects
- Audio: Shure SM7B through GoXLR Mini for narration (recorded separately, synced in post)
- Post: Premiere Pro + After Effects. Full color grade pushing the anamorphic look.

### Tier 2: "The Upperclassman" Weekly Series

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Purpose** | Retention engine — people follow for this character |
| **Frequency** | 1-2 per week |
| **Length** | 30-60 seconds |
| **Production** | 1-2 hours per video. Batch-shoot 3-4 in one session. |
| **Edit time** | 1-2 hours in Premiere |

**Structure:** Direct-to-camera hook (0-3s) → sketch cutaway that escalates absurdly (3-20s) → deadpan turn back to camera (20-30s) → natural DormWay beat (30-40s)

**DormWay presence:** Woven naturally into the character. "This is literally why I built the thing." Never "link in bio" energy.

**Production setup (one-time, reuse every session):**
- Camera: Canon R5 II + RF 85mm L DS at f/1.2-1.4 for creamy bokeh with DS softness. Tripod-locked, same framing every episode.
- Lighting: CB200C as key (~3800K warm), CB100C as edge/hair light, Nanoleafs/Govee for colored background accents. "Late night dorm" aesthetic — moody but flattering.
- Audio: SM7B through GoXLR Mini. Crisp, intimate, like talking to one person.
- Character: Riley at 110%. Not a bit — just heightened. Consistent wardrobe element for series recognition.

### Tier 3: "Cinematic Sketch" Tentpoles

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Purpose** | Event content for key calendar moments |
| **Frequency** | 1-2 per month at strategic moments |
| **Length** | 60-120 seconds |
| **Production** | Full-day shoot, full post-production |
| **Edit time** | Multi-day (portfolio pieces) |

**Structure:** Full narrative arc — acts, characters, payoff. Mini short films.

**DormWay presence:** The entire story is about the problem DormWay solves, told through cinema. The product is the context, not the CTA.

### Tier 2: Upperclassman Episode Concepts

Each episode follows the formula: Hook (0-3s) → Escalation cutaway (3-20s) → Deadpan turn (20-30s) → DormWay beat (30-40s).

#### Theme: The Panic Moments

**#1 "The 11pm Discovery"**
- Hook: "There's a moment every college student knows. It's 11pm. You're in bed. And your brain goes: *wait.*"
- Cutaway: Dramatic re-enactment — phone glow in dark room, Canvas loading, assignment due at midnight, speed-ramp frantic typing.
- Turn: "I had that moment 47 times freshman year. Then I stopped having it."
- Beat: Quick flash of DormWay "Heads Up" widget.

**#2 "Sunday Scaries: Academic Edition"**
- Hook: "Sunday 6pm. You open your laptop to 'just check what's due this week.' You do not close it until 2am."
- Cutaway: Time-lapse desk — tabs multiply, coffee appears, second coffee, notebook out, notebook thrown, phone call to mom, back to laptop.
- Turn: "The Sunday Scaries aren't about Monday. They're about not knowing what Monday wants from you."
- Beat: DormWay Today page — Monday's schedule, clear and finite.

#### Theme: The Systems Are Broken

**#3 "Canvas: A Love Letter"**
- Hook: "I love Canvas. Said no student, ever, in the history of higher education."
- Cutaway: Canvas UX speed-run — assignment under "Modules" not "Assignments," due date mismatch with syllabus, one professor uses Announcements, another email, a third carrier pigeon (commit to the bit — show an actual pigeon).
- Turn: "Canvas isn't bad at its job. Its job is just... not your job. Your job is knowing what's due. Canvas's job is making professors feel organized."
- Beat: "So I built the translator." → Schedule page.

**#4 "The Syllabus PDF"**
- Hook: "Your professor hands you a 47-page PDF in September and says 'everything you need is in here.' That's not a syllabus. That's a treasure map with no X."
- Cutaway: Print the syllabus, lay pages across floor like a crime scene. Highlighters, red string, magnifying glass. "Page 34, subsection C: 'participation will be assessed on an ongoing basis.' What does that MEAN?"
- Turn: "Or... you email it to us and we read it for you. In 30 seconds."
- Beat: Syllabus email → dashboard populated.

#### Theme: The Freshman Advice

**#5 "Week 3"**
- Hook: "Nobody warns you about Week 3."
- Cutaway: Week 1 — clean desk, color-coded notes, office hours for fun. Week 2 — slight cracks, one missed reading. Week 3 — full chaos, five things due, two unknown, one from a forgotten class.
- Turn: "Week 3 is when you find out if your system works. Most people don't have a system. They have vibes."
- Beat: "Vibes don't scale. This does." → Week view.

**#6 "The Five App Juggle"**
- Hook: "College students use an average of five apps to manage one semester. And none of them talk to each other."
- Cutaway: Split-screen chaos — Canvas, Google Calendar, email, Notes, syllabus PDF all open. Riley physically juggling objects (phone, laptop, notebook, coffee, PDF printout), dropping them one by one.
- Turn: "You know what talks to everything? One thing that has everything."
- Beat: Clean Today page. One screen.

#### Theme: The Relatable Rage

**#7 "Professor Email Roulette"**
- Hook: "One professor posts on Canvas. One emails. One uses Piazza. One posts on a website from 2003. One just... vibes."
- Cutaway: Checking every platform — different UIs, different logins, different notifications. One professor's "announcement" buried in a reply to a reply to a forwarded 2019 email thread.
- Turn: "I'm not mad at professors. They're doing their best. I'm mad at the infrastructure. So I replaced it."
- Beat: All courses, one dashboard.

**#8 "Group Project: A Horror Story"**
- Hook: "Four people. One Google Doc. Zero shared understanding of when anything is due."
- Cutaway: Horror movie treatment — Google Doc cursor moves on its own at 3am, someone deletes a paragraph, group chat has 47 unread messages all saying "when is this due," one person hasn't opened the doc and is presenting tomorrow.
- Turn: "Group projects fail because nobody has the same calendar. Wild concept."
- Beat: Shared schedule view.

#### Theme: The Self-Aware Meta

**#9 "Yes, I Made a Homework App"**
- Hook: "I had 100,000 followers on Twitch. I was a full-time streamer. And I gave it all up to build... a homework app."
- Cutaway: Old Twitch clips — gaming, laughing, community. Then reality: missing classes, missing deadlines, streaming setup collecting dust.
- Turn: "Sounds insane. It was insane. But the problem I was having? Every single student has it. So the homework app? It's not a homework app. It's the thing I needed to exist so I could do everything else."
- Beat: Brief DormWay flash. This one's about Riley.

**#10 "How to Game the Algorithm"**
- Hook: (Writing on whiteboard) "HOW TO GO VIRAL ON TIKTOK"
- Cutaway: Writing checklist — "Good hook ✓" "Relatable problem ✓" "Student founder angle ✓" "Show the app but make it seem organic ✓" "Pretend you're not selling anything ✓" "You're selling something ✓"
- Turn: Step back. "...yeah I'm definitely selling something. But it's free so does it count?"
- Beat: Shrug. "dormway.app. I'm not even charging you. What do you want from me."

## Production Workflow

### Batch Shoot System

**Session A: "The Desk" — every 4-6 weeks, one afternoon (~2-3 hours)**
- Set up Upperclassman rig once (85mm, CB200C key, CB100C edge, Nanoleafs, SM7B).
- Shoot all direct-to-camera A-roll for next 4-6 Tier 2 episodes back-to-back.
- Same shirt, same framing, same light. Consistency is the brand.
- Output: 4-6 weeks of A-roll banked.

**Session B: "The Cutaways" — same week as Session A, one afternoon (~3-4 hours)**
- Shoot all sketch cutaways and B-roll grouped by location, not by episode.
- Dorm room shots: desk chaos time-lapse, phone close-ups, laptop tabs, coffee accumulation, code on screen.
- Library/campus shots: students studying (wide anamorphic), tab-switching close-ups, golden hour walks.
- Phone/screen recordings: Canvas UX, syllabus email flow, DormWay dashboard.
- Output: Cutaway library for weeks of content.

**Session C: "The Hero" — 1-2 per month, half-day (~4-6 hours)**
- Dedicated shoot for one Tier 1 mockumentary piece.
- Full rig: anamorphics, Edelkrone HeadPLUS + JibONE, dramatic lighting, Godox flash.
- Shot list and storyboard prepared in advance.
- Output: One hero piece.

### Edit Pipeline

| Tier | Software | Time per piece | Notes |
|------|----------|---------------|-------|
| Tier 2 | Premiere Pro | 1-2 hours | Template the project file. A-roll → cutaway → A-roll → product beat. Text overlays, sound design, music. |
| Tier 1 | Premiere + After Effects | 4-8 hours | Full color grade pushing anamorphic look. B&W-to-color transitions. Genre-specific sound design. |
| Tier 3 | Premiere + After Effects | Multi-day | Portfolio pieces. Full post-production. |

### Screen Recording Setup

- Record DormWay web app (Today page, Schedule page) at native resolution on MacBook Pro M4 Max.
- Use Riley's real account with real course data (the current UI is polished and looks great on camera).
- QuickTime screen capture, crop in post to exact widget/section needed.
- For "reveal" moments: record full screen, use slow push-in keyframe (100% → 120% scale) to simulate camera move.
- Avoid filming iOS/macOS/iPadOS apps — those surfaces are rough and will be changing.

### Pre-Production Checklist (Hero Pieces)

Before every Tier 1 or Tier 3 shoot:

- [ ] Shot list / storyboard printed or on iPad
- [ ] Narration script written and rehearsed
- [ ] Music track selected and downloaded (edit rhythm planned)
- [ ] Locations scouted (lighting conditions, noise, permission if needed)
- [ ] DormWay account prepped with clean, realistic data for product shots
- [ ] Screen recordings captured (QuickTime, full resolution)
- [ ] All batteries charged (camera, gimbal, lights, phone, power bank)
- [ ] Memory cards formatted (LEXAR 2TB for the R5 II)
- [ ] Audio setup tested (SM7B levels, GoXLR routing)
- [ ] Wardrobe selected (consistent with character/series if applicable)

### Early Content as Experiments

The first 10 videos are explicitly experimental. Track what works and iterate:
- Does 30 seconds outperform 60 seconds?
- Does direct-to-camera outperform narration-over-B-roll?
- Which themes get the highest save rates?
- Does anamorphic widescreen stand out or confuse (pillarboxing on phone screens)?

After the first 10 videos, conduct a formal review: double down on top 2-3 formats, kill or rework the rest. The concept bank is a starting point, not a commitment.

**Realistic time commitment:**
- **Tier 2 only weeks:** ~4-6 hours (editing + posting + scheduling)
- **Hero production weeks:** ~12-16 hours (half-day shoot + 4-8 hours editing + Tier 2 maintenance)
- **Average across a month:** ~8-10 hours/week

This is a significant time investment from one of two founders. Content is secondary to product work — when they conflict, apply the degradation plan below.

### Degradation Plan (When Product Work Takes Priority)

When product emergencies, fundraising, or deadlines demand full attention:

1. **Level 1 — Light pressure:** Drop to 1 Tier 2/week instead of 2. Hero pieces pause. ~3 hours/week.
2. **Level 2 — Heavy pressure:** Post from banked content only. No new shooting or editing. ~30 min/week (scheduling).
3. **Level 3 — Full crunch:** Content pauses entirely. Resume when pressure lifts. The banked library means followers still have content to discover.

Hero pieces and tentpoles are **not** hard commitments — they shift to the next available window. Only the content calendar dates for tentpoles (May origin story, July welcome video) are strategically anchored and should be protected if at all possible.

### Audio / Music Sourcing

- **Tier 2 (weekly):** Royalty-free music from Artlist ($199/year, unlimited license for all platforms). Build a curated playlist of 10-15 tracks that match the "late night dorm" aesthetic. Reuse across episodes for sonic consistency.
- **Tier 1 (hero):** Genre-specific music from Artlist or Epidemic Sound. True crime score, heist theme, nature doc ambience. License before shooting so the edit rhythm matches the music.
- **Tier 3 (tentpoles):** Licensed tracks from Artlist or Musicbed. For "The Origin Story" specifically, the music choice is critical — budget time to find the right track before shooting.
- **Sound design:** Premiere Pro stock SFX for dramatic stings, transition sounds, comedic beats. After Effects for anything custom.
- **Platform safety:** Artlist and Epidemic Sound tracks are pre-cleared for Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube. No takedown risk. Never use copyrighted popular music.

### Account Strategy

Post from **Riley's personal accounts** (@imaginerc on Instagram, TikTok, Twitter), not a DormWay brand account.

Rationale:
- The entire strategy is built on Riley's personality, filmmaking, and founder story. A brand account strips that away.
- Personal accounts have existing followers (58K TikTok, etc.) — don't split the audience.
- Gen Z follows people, not brands. A "DormWay" account posting the same content would feel corporate.
- When DormWay needs a brand account later (campus-specific content, customer support, etc.), it can exist separately. For now, Riley IS the brand.

Bio on all platforms: "Never wonder what's due. → dormway.app"

## Distribution Strategy

### Platform Priority

1. **Instagram Reels (primary):** Target audience lives here. Cinematic anamorphic look stands out on Instagram where average production quality is higher — content looks *right*, not overproduced.
2. **TikTok (secondary):** Cross-post everything. Let TikTok be where things go viral accidentally, not the optimization target.
3. **YouTube Shorts (passive):** Cross-post. Save Tier 3 tentpoles as full YouTube videos (not Shorts) for permanent portfolio/pitch use.
4. **Twitter/X (founder narrative):** Not video-first. Behind-the-scenes stills, one-liner observations, "building in public" text posts.

### Posting Cadence

| Week Type | Monday | Thursday | Ad Hoc |
|-----------|--------|----------|--------|
| Normal | Tier 2 episode | Tier 2 episode or BTS still | Instagram Stories (shooting BTS, campus life) |
| Hero week | Tier 1 hero piece | Tier 2 episode | Behind-the-scenes of making the hero piece |
| Tentpole week | Tentpole drops | Follow-up / reaction content | Push across all platforms |

### Posting Times
- **Instagram Reels:** Monday 5-7pm, Thursday 5-7pm (aligned with posting cadence above). Sunday 6-8pm for Sunday Scaries-themed content.
- **TikTok:** Same content, cross-posted 2-3 hours later.
- **Stories:** Daily when shooting — BTS is free engagement.

### The Funnel

- **In video:** "dormway.app" as text in last 3-5 seconds. Casual if spoken. Never "link in bio." Never "download now."
- **In bio:** "Never wonder what's due. → dormway.app" on every platform.
- **In comments:** Pin: "yes it's free, yes it works, dormway.app" — self-aware, matches the voice.

### Content Recycling
- Hero pieces → 2-3 shorter clips for Stories and secondary posts.
- Behind-the-scenes stills → Twitter/X posts.
- Best-performing Tier 2 → "remixed" (same premise, different execution) after 4-6 weeks.
- Underperforming Tier 2 → cutaway footage reused in future edits.

## Success Metrics

### Content Metrics (track weekly)
- Views per video (by tier and topic)
- Save rate (strongest signal of "I'll come back to this")
- Share rate (strongest signal of virality)
- Comment volume and sentiment
- Follower growth rate

### Funnel Metrics (track weekly)
- dormway.app visits from social — use platform-specific landing pages (dormway.app/ig, dormway.app/tiktok) or UTM-tagged links in bio to enable source attribution. In-video text just shows "dormway.app" (clean, no ugly UTM strings).
- Source-attributed first actions (per [GTM Playbook - Spring 2026 (Current)](/docs/product/go-to-market/gtm-playbook-spring-2026-current))
- Conversion: view → site visit → first action
- Consider adding a post-signup "How did you hear about us?" prompt to capture attribution that link tracking misses.

### Quality Signals
- Does the content pass the Riley Test? (Would he send to a friend?)
- Is DormWay presence ≤15 seconds per video?
- Are comments about the content ("this is so true") or the ad ("another startup ad")?

### Kill Criteria
Stop or pivot if:
- Save rate below 2% consistently after 10+ videos
- Comments skew "ad" over "relatable"
- Time investment exceeds 8 hours/week without corresponding funnel signal
- Content production interferes with product development priorities
