# Class Treasury Sponsorship Strategy (Current)

## Strategic Decision

DormWay should **not** use class treasury sponsorship as its core monetization engine.

Class treasury should be used as:
- an activation and growth campaign,
- a class-wide unlock mechanism,
- a supplemental revenue stream.

Core long-term monetization should remain:
1. Recurring org subscriptions.
2. Campus/department sponsorship or institutional funding.
3. Treasury campaigns as additive upside.

## Why This Is Best for Long-Term DormWay

Treasury-only dependence creates strategic risk:
- Revenue is seasonal and volatile.
- Campaign misses create planning instability.
- Hard to forecast headcount and roadmap investment.
- Optics can drift toward "crowdfunding basic student tooling."

A mixed model is stronger:
- Recurring contracts fund platform reliability and product velocity.
- Treasury campaigns create bursts of adoption and goodwill.
- Campus matching programs de-risk all-or-nothing thresholds.

## Recommended Treasury Model

### Campaign Type
- **All-or-nothing class unlock** (approved direction).
- Example: "UMich Class of 2026 unlock."

### Funding Unit
- Target: **$5.50 per student-equivalent per semester**.
- Goal formula:
  - `goal = graduating_class_size * 5.50 * buffer`
  - buffer default: `1.12` (fees + refund/ops variance).

### Payment Flow
- Pledge now, charge only if unlock threshold is reached.
- If threshold is not reached, no charge.

### Overfunding Policy
- Primary: roll over to next term for same class.
- Secondary option: allocate a capped share to a cross-campus sponsorship pool.

## Messaging and Positioning

Primary message:
- "DormWay remains free for students."
- "Families, alumni, and university partners can fund class-wide premium coordination."

Avoid:
- Messaging that implies parent visibility into student private schedules.
- Language that frames this as replacing base student access.

Preferred framing options:
1. Class Legacy Gift.
2. Parent-Powered Student Support.
3. Parent + University Match for Class Unlock.

## Stakeholder Reactions to Anticipate

### Students
- Likely positive on free access and class-wide unlock.
- Sensitive to fairness and privacy optics.

### Parents/Families
- Respond well to concrete impact math and low-dollar asks.
- Higher conversion with visible progress + matching commitments.

### University/Student Life
- Interested if privacy and FERPA posture are clear.
- More likely to support as co-funding or gap-closure partner.

## Risks and Mitigations

### Risk: Equity optics ("richer classes unlock more often")
- Mitigation: university match pool + bridge sponsor model near threshold.

### Risk: Campaign miss damages momentum
- Mitigation: pre-commit anchor sponsors before public launch; soft-launch with pledges.

### Risk: Trust concerns around money use
- Mitigation: public treasury ledger (goal, raised, fees, net, rollover rules).

### Risk: Compliance/messaging issues
- Mitigation: legal-reviewed campaign language and clear non-tax advice unless verified.

## Operating Model Recommendation

### Revenue Role by Horizon
- Near-term: treasury as activation + social proof while org subscriptions scale.
- Mid-term: treasury + campus match as expansion lever.
- Long-term: treasury remains optional campaign layer, not required for core operations.

### Planning Rule
- Do not commit fixed operating spend that depends on one treasury campaign succeeding.
- Treat treasury upside as incremental growth budget.

## Key Metrics

Campaign metrics:
- Pledge conversion rate.
- % goal reached by day 7/day 14.
- Final unlock rate.
- Average contribution and contribution distribution.

Business metrics:
- Class activation lift after unlock.
- Retention delta unlocked vs non-unlocked cohorts.
- Revenue mix share from treasury vs recurring streams.

## Revenue Mix Guardrail (Suggested)

Target steady-state revenue composition:
1. 50-70% recurring org/campus revenue.
2. 10-30% institutional partnerships and matching programs.
3. 10-25% treasury campaign revenue.

If treasury exceeds this range, treat as upside and reinvest conservatively.
