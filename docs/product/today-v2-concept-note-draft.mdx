# Today V2 Concept Note

Future direction for DormWay's Today surface. This is a concept note, not a build spec. Nothing here is current scope. It captures the strongest ideas from initial product review and ideation so they survive as a reference for future iterations.

**Status:** Draft. Do not implement from this doc. Do not treat as committed strategy.

**Governed by:** [Today Screen Product Principles (Current)](/docs/product/today-screen-product-principles-current) — any future version must still satisfy those principles.

---

## Core Shift

The current Today screen answers: **"What does your day look like?"**

The future Today screen should answer: **"Am I okay? What do I need to deal with?"**

This is the difference between a display and an awareness system. A display shows you your schedule. An awareness system tells you whether something needs your attention, what changed since you last checked, and gives you confidence that you're not forgetting anything.

The underlying product truth: students don't open DormWay to see a pretty timeline. They open it because they're anxious about forgetting something, or they need to know what's next in 30 seconds. The screen must resolve that anxiety, not just present information.

---

## Why This Matters for Real Students

College is not one system. It's dozens of inconsistent systems layered on top of each other:

- Every professor communicates differently (Canvas, email, verbal, their own website, paper handouts)
- Every class has different rhythms (weekly problem sets vs. two midterms and a final vs. ongoing project work)
- Every student has different capacity (course load, jobs, athletics, executive function, mental health)
- Everything changes without warning (deadlines move, classes cancel, assignments appear, professors contradict their syllabi)

The enemy is not "I don't have a calendar." It's **"I don't know if I'm forgetting something."** That uncertainty is what causes the 3am anxiety, the Sunday-night dread, the between-class phone check.

A second brain for college admin doesn't need to be smart. It needs to be **trustworthy and complete** — holding the full picture so the student doesn't have to hold it in their head.

## Five Concept Directions

### 1. Awareness Over Display

**Current:** The Today screen shows your day chronologically. Classes, then nudges, then coming-up items, in time order.

**Future:** The Today screen leads with what needs your attention. Due items, changes, conflicts, and risks appear first. The chronological timeline is the supporting backbone, not the primary structure.

**What this looks like:**
- An "attention stack" at the top of the screen: the 1-3 things that actually need action, ranked by urgency
- Each attention item has a clear next action (tap to see detail, mark done, dismiss)
- The timeline sits below as orientation context
- When there are zero attention items, that state feels like a reward: "You're clear"

**Why it matters:** A student with 4 classes and 3 things due doesn't need an 8am-10pm timeline. They need to know: "What do I have to deal with before I can relax?"

### 2. What Changed Since Last Check

**Current:** The Today screen shows the current state. There's no concept of "new since you last looked."

**Future:** The screen surfaces changes — a deadline that moved, a new assignment that appeared, a class that was canceled, a conflict that was resolved. A quiet "1 update" indicator that expands to show what shifted.

**What this looks like:**
- A change indicator (badge or expandable section) showing items that are new or modified since the student's last session
- Changes are distinguished from stable items visually
- The student can acknowledge changes to clear the indicator

**Why it matters:** The biggest source of missed deadlines isn't "I never knew" — it's "I knew, but then it changed and I didn't notice." Change detection is how DormWay earns the right to be a second brain. If DormWay can reliably tell you "this moved," you stop needing to manually re-check Canvas.

### 3. Adaptive Density Based on Load

**Current:** The Today screen shows the same structure regardless of how much is happening — hero + timeline + (currently) swipe stack.

**Future:** The screen adapts its density to the student's actual load.

**What this looks like:**
- **Light day** (0-1 classes, nothing due): Big calm hero, minimal timeline, maybe just "Nothing needs attention today." The screen is small and feels good.
- **Normal day** (2-3 classes, a couple things due): Hero + attention items + timeline. Standard view.
- **Heavy day** (4+ classes, multiple deadlines, conflicts): Tighter layout, more information-dense, priority stack prominent, timeline compressed.
- **Overwhelm state** (many overdue items, multiple conflicts): Different posture entirely. Not "here are your 12 problems" but "here's the ONE thing to do next." Break the paralysis.

**Why it matters:** A screen that looks the same on a light Saturday and during midterm week is not adapting to the student's reality. The density of the screen should reflect the density of the day. Light days should feel calm. Heavy days should feel organized, not chaotic.

### 4. User-Set Preferences That Teach the System

**Current:** The Today screen is one-size-fits-all.

**Future:** Students can set lightweight preferences that change how DormWay surfaces information — and DormWay learns from those preferences over time.

**What preferences might look like:**
- "Show me deadlines 3 days early / 1 day early / day of" — urgency sensitivity
- "This class matters more to me" — per-course priority weighting
- "Stop showing me study block suggestions" — content type control
- "I don't need weather on my today screen" — section visibility
- Implicit learning: if a student always dismisses a certain type of nudge, DormWay stops showing it. If they always check tasks at 10pm, DormWay optimizes the evening view for task triage.

**What this does NOT look like:**
- A settings page with 40 toggles
- Complex personalization that requires setup before value
- An onboarding flow that asks layout questions before showing anything useful

**What it DOES support:**
- Students who want to rearrange, resize, or hide sections of Today can do so
- On iPad/Mac, widget-level spatial arrangement (timeline + tasks side by side, etc.)
- The customization capability exists in the architecture but never gates first value

**The principle:** The default must be great. Customization is available, never required. The default is a tall vertical timeline that works for most students without any setup. Students who want to adapt the screen to how their brain works — which courses are prominent, how big the timeline is, whether weather shows — can. The system also learns from behavior and offers contextual controls ("You keep dismissing these. Want us to stop?").

**Why it matters:** Every student is different. Different majors, different professors, different rhythms, different executive function capacity. One layout cannot serve all of them equally. But asking students to configure their experience upfront creates friction. The right model is: start with a great default, let students customize if they want, observe behavior, offer lightweight tuning, adapt. The students who care enough to customize get a meaningfully better experience. The students who don't still get a great one.

### 5. Same Brain, Different Density by Platform

**Current:** iPhone has a fused timeline. Web has a 5-widget batch system. These are architecturally and conceptually different.

**Future:** The same awareness model — same priority logic, same attention stack, same data — shaped differently for each platform.

**What this looks like:**
- **iPhone:** Single column. Glanceable. 5-15 second check. Hero + attention items + compact timeline. Optimized for checking between classes.
- **iPad:** Two-column option. Today's attention stack on one side, timeline or task management on the other. More context visible at once. Suited for study desk sessions.
- **Mac:** Full density. Today + week view + task management visible simultaneously. Keyboard-navigable. Planning-session optimized.
- **Watch:** Status only. Hero state as a complication. "Free until 2:30 · 2 due." The most compressed form.

**The constraint:** The underlying data model and priority logic must be shared across platforms. The *view* adapts to the device. A student who checks iPhone between classes and iPad at their desk should see the same brain, at different zoom levels.

**Why it matters:** Students use multiple devices. If Today is a completely different experience on each one, it breaks the mental model. If it's the exact same single-column layout stretched to a 13" screen, it wastes the opportunity.

## What This Is Not

- Not a build spec — none of this is scoped for implementation
- Not a promise — these are directions to explore, not commitments
- Not a replacement for the current screen — the current GPS Hero + Timeline can ship and be good. These ideas layer on later.
- Not an AI personalization pitch — the value is in accurate awareness, not in generative features
- Not an argument against customization — customization is important, but it should complement awareness, not replace it

---

## Relationship to Current Implementation

The current DayPlan Today screen (GPS Hero + Smart Timeline) is a good foundation. The five concepts above build on that foundation:

1. **Awareness over display** → promote DueTodayBanner and attention items above the timeline, not just inside it
2. **What changed** → add a change-detection layer to the DayPlan data (requires backend: diff current DayPlan against previous version)
3. **Adaptive density** → vary the layout structure based on `dueToday.count`, `hardCommitments.count`, and day load signals already in the DayPlan
4. **Preferences** → start with 2-3 user-facing controls (urgency sensitivity, course priority, section visibility), then expand based on usage data
5. **Platform density** → shared ViewModel/data layer, platform-specific views (already partially true with the web widget system)

None of these require throwing away the current work. They require evolving it in a specific direction.

---

## Open Questions

- How much change detection is possible with current data? DayPlan is regenerated daily at 5 AM — can we diff today's plan against yesterday's to surface changes?
- What's the minimum viable preference set? Which 2-3 controls would matter most to students?
- Does adaptive density require backend support (day-load classification) or can the client compute it from existing DayPlan fields?
- How do we validate that students actually want priority-before-chronology, vs. preferring a familiar timeline?
- What does the "overwhelm state" actually look like? Is breaking paralysis a real product job, or does it risk being patronizing?
