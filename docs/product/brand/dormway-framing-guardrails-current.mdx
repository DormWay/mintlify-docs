# DormWay Framing Guardrails

Short agent-facing extract of the key framing rules. For the full brand system, see [DormWay-Brand-Voice-and-Positioning-Master-Guide](/docs/product/brand/dormway-brand-voice-and-positioning-master-guide).

## Primary Framing

DormWay is **crisis prevention infrastructure for college students**.

- Core promise: "Never wonder what's due."
- Mechanism: Upload syllabus, connect Canvas, see everything in one place.
- Emotional benefit: Sleep without the 3am "did I forget something?" anxiety.
- Authenticity: Built by Riley, a 22-year-old student who needed it to survive his own coursework.

## Anti-Framing

DormWay should never sound like:

- A corporate EdTech platform
- An AI company that happens to do education
- A productivity/optimization tool for overachievers
- A social network or student community app
- A replacement for Canvas or university systems

## Voice Rules

- Human, student-native, concrete, trust-forward
- Friendly first, credible second, never corporate
- Lead with student pain, not features
- Specific scenarios over abstract benefits ("Ever realize at 11pm something's due?" not "Optimize your academic workflow")

## AI Language Rules

- AI should feel like magic, not marketing
- Never lead with "AI-powered" — focus on outcomes
- Only use AI wording as a trust boundary: "We use AI for dates, not essays"
- Say "Ace" or "chat assistant", not "AI assistant"
- No: AI-powered, intelligent assistant, digital companion, AI study buddy

## Banned Patterns

- Corporate buzzwords: synergize, optimize, leverage, paradigm, ecosystem, holistic, next-gen, revolutionary, disruptive, game-changer, cutting-edge, comprehensive, platform (when used corporately)
- Overclaiming: anything that implies DormWay writes essays, does homework, guarantees grades, or replaces studying
- Hype framing: "revolutionary", "best-in-class", "next-generation"
- Vague promises: "optimize your semester", "command center", "autopilot"
- Monetization drift: "free forever" or "no premium tiers" (use "free for students" at the core level)

## The Riley Test (Quick Version)

Before publishing anything student-facing:

1. **Friend test:** Would Riley send this to a friend?
2. **Authenticity test:** Does this sound like a real student wrote it?
3. **Jargon test:** Any corporate buzzwords?
4. **Problem-first test:** Do we lead with student pain or our features?
5. **Humility test:** Are we being helpful or bragging?
6. **Transparency test:** Are we being upfront about how this works?

If any answer is wrong, rewrite before publishing.

## What Is Off-Brand

- Messaging that leads with features or technology instead of student problems
- Copy that sounds like it was written by a marketing team, not a student
- Claims that imply DormWay replaces studying, guarantees outcomes, or automates coursework
- Language that positions DormWay as enterprise software or a "platform"
